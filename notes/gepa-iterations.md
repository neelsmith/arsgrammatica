`optimize_gepa.py` doesn't pick a fixed number of iterations itself — it hands GEPA a *budget*, and GEPA runs as many iterations as fit inside that budget. Two different counters were visible in your crash log, and they mean different things:

The `264/444 rollouts` in the tqdm bar is the metric-call budget — GEPA calls `syntax_metric` once per (candidate program, example) pair, and each such call is one "rollout." `Iteration 15` is GEPA's own outer loop counter: each iteration proposes one new candidate instruction (via the reflection LM reading feedback), tries it on a minibatch of the trainset, and — every few iterations — runs a full evaluation on the valset to check whether it's actually better. So iterations consume a variable number of rollouts each; there's no fixed iteration count set in advance, only a rollout ceiling that the loop stops at (or, in your case, crashed before reaching).

Where does 444 come from? I checked `dspy`'s own `auto_budget()` formula, since you ran with the default `--auto light`. It depends on three things: the preset (`light`→6, `medium`→12, `heavy`→18 "candidates" internally), the number of optimizable components in the program (`analyze` has exactly one predictor, so this is 1), and the size of your valset (or trainset, if you pass `--val-fraction 0`). Working the formula backward from your numbers, your run's valset came out to 16 examples — consistent with your `data/vulgate` pool landing around 80 examples total once split with the default `--val-fraction 0.2`. Concretely, the formula is: an initial full valset eval, plus `num_candidates × 5` bootstrapping trials, plus `N × 35` minibatch evaluations (where `N` is derived from the preset and predictor count via `max(2 × 2 × preds × log2(num_candidates), 1.5 × num_candidates)`), plus periodic full-valset re-checks roughly every 5 iterations. For your setup that adds up to exactly 444.

The practical upshot: this scales directly with how much data you point `--eval-dir` at, since valset size is a multiplied term in that formula (the periodic full-eval component alone is `~3 × valset_size` at `light`). A bigger corpus doesn't just make training richer, it makes every full-eval checkpoint proportionally more expensive too — this is a good part of why your run stretched to 15+ minutes before dying, versus the original 17-example runs being quick. `--auto medium`/`heavy` would push `num_candidates` (and so `N`) higher still, multiplying the total further.

If you want a predictable, fixed number of rollouts instead of whatever the preset's formula produces for your current pool size, `--max-metric-calls N` bypasses `auto_budget()` entirely and just runs until it's made `N` metric calls — useful for planning a run's length up front rather than discovering it only after `dspy` logs "Running GEPA for approx N metric calls" at the start.
