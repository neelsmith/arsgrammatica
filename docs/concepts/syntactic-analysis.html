arsgrammatica’s graph of syntactic relations

Note

In arsgrammatica the TokenAnalysis class is the primary impementation of these ideas.

When arsgrammatica analyses a passage of Latin, every non-punctuation token is related to one or two other tokens in a graph structure. This page summarizes all relationship categories that arsgrammatica recognizes.

Constructions of verbal expressions

Independent clause

Verbs of independent (main) clauses are related to a hypthetical root value as unit verb.

Example: arma virumque cano.

Token relationship related token
cano unit verb (root)

Directly quoted speech

Independent verbs in directly quoted speech are related to the verb introducing the quotation as direct quote.

Example: ‘hanc ego aram’ inquit ‘Pudicitiae plebeiae dedico’.

Token relationship related token
dedico direct quote inquit
inquit unit verb (root)

Aside

When the syntax of the sentence is interrupted by an independent verbal expression, the verb of the interrupting expression is related to the verb of the interrupted context as aside.

Example: Sp. Nautius – Octavium Maecium quidam eum tradunt – cum auxiliaribus cohortibus erat.

Token relationship related token
tradunt aside erat
erat unit verb (root)

Dependent clause

Verbs of dependent clauses are related to a subordinating word (such as a conjunction or relative pronoun) as unit verb.

Example: Hercules cum gregem perlustrasset, pergit ad proximam speluncam.

Token relationship related token
perlustrasset unit verb cum
pergit unit verb (root)

Indirect statement

Infinitive forms in indirect statement relate to the verb of speaking as indirect statement.

Example: id philtrum esse dixit.

Token relationship related token
esse indirect statement dixit
dixit unit verb (root)

Circumstantial participle

Circumstantial participles express a verbal idea that is related to an antecedent substantive in the relation circumstantial participle.

Example: adveniens vir Tarquiniique excepti benigne.

Token relationship related token
adveniens circumstantial participle vir
excepti unit verb (root)
(elided sum) excepti auxiliary

Other constructions with verbs

Complementary infinitive

When an infinitive is used to complete the semantics of a verbal expression with certain verbs such as volo, the infinitive relates to the verbal expression as complementary infinitive.

Example: ascribi se in eam civitatem voluit.

Token relationship related token
ascribi complementary infinitive voluit
voluit unit verb (root)

Compound verb forms

Compound forms using sum with a participle treat the participle as the verb of the verbal unit and the form of sum as an auxiliary. The participle therefore is related to its context just as any other verbal expression would be. The form of sum relates to the participle as auxiliary.

Example: urbs condita est.

Token relationship related token
condita unit verb (root)
est auxiliary condita

A-A-T relations

The Agent-Action-Target model focuses on two special relations to verbal expressions: the agent and the target. In Latin (as in English), these relations are constructed differently depending on the voice of the verbal expression. Agency is expressed by the subject of transitive active, intransitive, and linking verbs, and by the ablative of agent construction with passive verbs. Target status is expressed by the direct object of transitive active verbs, the predicate of linking verbs, and by the subject of transitive passive verbs.

Subject

A substantive functioning as the grammatical subject of a verbal expression is related to it as subject.

Example: Lucumo Demarati Corinthii filius erat.

Token relationship related token
Lucumo subject erat
erat unit verb (root)

Agent

The headword of a prepositional phrase expressing agency with passive verbs (that is, a/ab) relates to the verbal expression as agent.

Example: urbs a Romulo condita est.

Token relationship related token
urbs subject condita
a agent condita
Romulo object of preposition a
condita unit verb (root)
est auxiliary condita

See below on objects of prepositions.

Direct object

The direct object of a transitive active verb is related to the verbal expression as direct object.

Example: Tarquinium mala libido Lucretiae per vim stuprandae capit.

Token relationship related token
Tarquinium direct object capit
libido subject capit
capit unit verb (root)

Predicate

Substantives or adjectives serving as predicates of linking verbs are related to the verb as predicate

Example: Lucumo Demarati Corinthii filius erat.

Token relationship related token
Lucumo subject erat
filius predicate erat
erat unit verb (root)

Case constructions with substantives

Apart from the special A-A-T relations, arsgrammatica does not subdivide syntactic constructions of substantives by their semantics. Instead, it identifies the case that constitutes the syntactic relationship. arsgrammatica leaves it to subsequent semantic analysis to decide if, for example, a genitive relation between two nouns expresses possession, a partitive idea, material or units of measure, or whether the relationship is a subjective or objective action.

Genitive

When a substantive in the genitive case modifies another substantive, the relationship is genitive.

Example: hic filius erat regis.

Token relationship related token
hic subject erat
filius predicate erat
erat unit verb (root)
regis genitive filius

Dative

When a substantive in the dative case is related to a verb or another substantive, the relationship is dative.

Example of relationship to verb: documentum mortalibus dedero.

Token relationship
mortalibus dative dedero
dedero unit verb (root)

Example of relationship to a substantive: te mihi mater aucturum caelestium numerum cecinit

Token relationship
mihi dative mater
mater subject cecinit
cecinit unit verb (root)

Accusative

When an accusative noun is related to a noun, or when it relates to a verb but does not express a direct object relationship, the relationship value is accusative.

Example: Romam venit.

The verb venit is intransitive, but Romam relates to it.

Token relationship related token
Romam accusative venit
venit unit verb (root)

Example: duo milia passuum iter fecerunt.

The phrase duo milia passuum qualifies the noun iter with a length of distance specified in the accusative.

Token relationship related token
milia accusative iter
passuum genitive milia
iter direct object fecerunt
fecerunt unit verb (root)

Ablative

When nouns in the ablative case are related to another noun or to a verb, the relationship is ablative.

Example: omnia ferro flammaque miscet

Token relationship related token
ferro ablative miscet
flamma ablative miscet
miscet unit verb (root)

Example: Collatinus negat verbis opus esse.

Token relationship related token
Collatinus subject negat
negat unit verb (root)
verbis ablative opus
opus subject esse
esse indirect statement negat

Vocative

Nouns in the vocative case are related to a verbal expression as vocative.

Example: Non est ita, domine.

Token relationship related token
est unit verb (root)
domine vocative est

Substantives from verbal roots

In addition to the previously discussed constructions, the infinitive may be used as a nominative singular noun naming the action of a verbal idea (e.g., amare = “the act of loving”). In the oblique cases, the gerund is used to name the action of a verbal idea. The syntactic relationships of these substantive forms are treated exactly the same way as any other substantives.

Example: dolere malum est.

The infinitive dolere is the subject of the verb est.

Token relationship related token
dolere subject est
malum predicate est
est unit verb (root)

As in all infinitive constructions, the infinitive, like any other verbal form, may have objects; if a subject is explicitly given, it will be in the accusative case.

Example: Ingenuas didicisse fideliter artes emollit mores.

Token relationship related token
didicisse subject emollit
artes direct object didicisse
emollit unit verb (root)
mores direct object emollit

Example: Multo labore constat ars dicendi.

Token relationship related token
labore ablative constat
constat unit verb (root)
ars subject constat
dicendi genitive ars

Adverbial and adjectival expressions

Adverbs

Adverbs and other adverbial expressions have relationship of adverbial to a verbal expression.

Example: ad Ianiculum forte ventum erat.

Token relationship related token
forte adverbial ventum
ventum unit verb (root)
erat auxiliary erat

Adjectives

If an adjective is used as a substantive, it is treated just like any other substantive. If it describes a substantive, it has the relationship adjectival to that substantive. This example illustrates both an adjective used substantively (bonorum) and an adjective used adjectivally (omnium)

Example: Lucumo superfuit patri bonorum omnium heres,

Token relationship related token
Lucumo subject superfuit
superfuit unit verb (root)
bonorum genitive heres
omnium adjectival bonorum
heres predicate superfuit

Prepositional phrases

Prepositional phrases most frequently functional adverbially. In this construction the preposition is related to a verb or adjective with the adverbial relationship. Less frequently, a prepositional phrase serves as an adjectival description of a substantive. In these constructions, the preposition relates to the substantive as adjectival. Nouns functioning as objects of prepositions, whatever their case, relate to the preposition as object of preposition.

Example of an adverbial phrase: ad Ianiculum forte ventum erat.

Token relationship related token
ad adverbial ventum
Ianiculum object of preposition ad
forte adverbial ventum
ventum unit verb (root)
erat auxiliary erat

Example of an adjectival phrase: nuntius victoriae ad Cannas Carthaginem venerat

Token relationship related token
nuntius subject venerat
victoriae genitive nuntius
ad adjectival victoriae
Cannas object of preposition ad
Carthaginem accusative venerat
venerate unit verb (root)

Special substantive relations

Ablative absolute

Substantives modified by a participle can be used in the ablative case without other syntactic connection to the governing verbal expression (that is, they are syntactically absolute). In this construction, the noun has the relationship ablative absolute to the governing verb. The participle is treated as any other circumstantial participle.

Example: Anco regnante Lucumo Romam commigravit.

Token relationship related token
Anco ablative absolute commigravit
regnante circumstantial participle Anco
Lucumo subject commigravit
Romam accusative commigravit
commigravit unit verb (root)

Apposition

When a substantive stands in apposition to another substantive, it has the relationship apposition; the first substantive is constructed normally.

Example: .Neptunus et Aegeus Pandionis filius in fano Mineruae cum Aethra Pitthei filia una nocte concubuerunt.

Token relationship related token
Neptunus subject concubuerunt
Aegeus subject concubuerunt
Pandionis genitive filius
filius apposition Aegeus
cum adverbial concubuerunt
Aethra object of preposition cum
Pitthei genitive filia
*filia apposition Aethra
una adjectival nocte
nocte ablative concubuerunt
concubuerunt unit verb (root)

Praenomen

A token that arsgrammatica classifies as praenomen is an abbreviation used in conjunction wth a fully spelled out nomen or cognomen. The abbreviated praenomen relates to the full form clearly showing its case usage as praenomen.

Example: Sp. Nautius – Octavium Maecium quidam eum tradunt – cum auxiliaribus cohortibus erat.

Token relationship related token
Sp. praenomen Nautius
Nautius subject erat
erat unit verb (root)

Syntactic dependencies of implied tokens

The discussion of tokenization introduced the idea of implied tokens.

We treat their syntactic relations in exactly the same way as if they were instantiated by a real token in the text. Here is a summary of possibilities:

implied sum, continued verb, implied substantive

…..

arsgrammatica recognizes two categories of understood or implied verbal expressions.

  1. elided form of sum: the verb sum (“to be”) is often elided in the present tense. A new token with unique ID must be added to the token table, and an entry added to the list of verbal expressions. The token will have None for its text value. Example: omnia praeclara rara is a complete sentence with subject omnia praeclara and predicate rara. A new token will be created with None as its text value, and its ID will be entered in the list of verbal expressions. This predicate construction is a main sentence, so its syntactic type will be independent clause and the semantic type will be linking verb. This elision can also occur with multiword compounds such as the perfect passive system, but here the treatment differs from the bare-predicate case above: because the participle itself is a real, already-present token, it – not a newly created token – anchors the verbal expression, per the multi-word compound verb form rule above. Example: the sentence inde P. Valerius iterum T. Lucretius consules facti. has a single verbal expression with a perfect passive verb, facti with sunt omitted. facti itself is entered in the list of verbal expressions (syntactic type independent clause, semantic type transitive passive). We still add a new token with unique ID and None for its text value to record the elided sunt, but this new token is not itself a verbal expression: it relates to facti as its auxiliary, exactly as a written-out sunt would. One important category of elided forms of sum is the participle: since there is no present participle of sum, its presence is always implied only. Example: in the sentence P. Valerius anno post Agrippa Menenio P. Postumio consulibus moritur, the phrase Agrippa Menenio P. Postumio consulibus is a predicate construction implying a circumstantial participle of “to be”. Here there is no already-present participle to anchor the verbal expression (unlike facti above), so we must add a new token with unique ID to the token table and use its ID for a new entry in the list of verbal expressions. The syntactic type will be circumstantial participle and the semantic type will be linking verb since this is a predicate construction (“when Agrippa Menenius and Publius Postumius were consuls…”)

  2. continuation of indirect discourse: in long sections of indirect speech, entire passages may appear without repeating any governing verb of speaking or thinking. In this situation, we must add new token with unique ID to the token table, with None for its text value, and use its ID for new entry in the list of verbal expressions. For example, these three verbal expressions are each constructed in indirect statement: nimium Tarquinios regno adsuesse; initium a Prisco factum; regnasse dein Ser. Tullium. They imply a verb of speaking governing all three verbal expressions, so we add a new token with unique ID to the list of tokens, and add a verbal expression with its ID to the list of verbal expressions. Here, we assume that the understood verbal expression is of syntatic type independent clause; its semantic type is ‘transitive active’.
