Verbal units

Note

In arsgrammatica the VerbalExpression class is the primary implementation of these ideas.

Although arsgrammatica tries to account for all syntactic features of a Latin text, its analytical scheme is anchored in the reduced Agent-Action-Target model (AAT): all syntactic features are organized around explicit or implicit verbal ideas, that may optionally include agent/target elements (e.g., for transitive active verbs, subject/direct object elements). These verbal expressions (subject-verb ideas) most frequently correspond to clauses in an English translation. (Of course in Latin the subject may be implicit where that is not possible in English.)

Verbal constructions

arsgrammatica identifies three possible constructions as verbal expressions: finite verbs, infinitives and participles.

  1. Every finite verb constitutes a verbal expression. Latin finite verbs include the compound forms of the perfect and pluperfect tenses (composed of a past participle plus a form of sum) as well as conjugated verbs forms identifiable by tense-mood-voice-person-number.
  2. Infinitives constitute a verbal expression when they are constructed as a verbal expression in indirect speech.
  3. Participles constitute a verbal expression when they have a circumstantial sense rather than purely attributive sense.

arsgrammatica classifies verbal expressions according to syntactic type and semantic type.

Syntactic type

independent

Also called “main” or “principal” verbs, these are syntactically independent finite verbs: their clause is syntactically coherent by itself.

Example: Quo usque tandem abutere, Catilina, patientia nostra?

abutere is an independent verbal expresion.

dependent

Finite verbs introduced by a subordinating word (such as a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun). They cannot appear without an explicit or implicit governing (superior) clause.

Example: principes Albanorum in patres, ut ea quoque pars rei publicae cresceret, legit.

The verb legit is an independent verbal expression; cresceret is dependent (introduced by the subordinating conjunction ut).

direct quote

Finite verbs in directly quoted speech.

Example: Tuum est’ inquit, ‘Servi regnum.’

inquit is an independent verbal unit, while est occurs in directly quoted speech and is classifed as direct quote.

aside

A finite verb that interrupts the syntactic flow of the surrounding text.

Example: equidem — pace dixerim deum — eos nos iam populi Romani beneficio esse spero.

spero is independent, the infinitive esse is part of an indirect statement (below). dixerim is an aside, as the entire phrase pace dixerim deum interrupts the sentence with a syntactically unconneccted verbal expression.

indirect statement

An infinitive construction in indirect speech.

Example: Collatinus negat verbis opus esse.

negat is indepedendent; the infinitive esse is indirect speech.

Note that as with finite verbs, compound verbs constructed from participles plus “to be” use the participle as the verbal expression. (In the token graph, the form of “to be” will related to it as an auxiliary.)

Example: facturum enim se fuisse dixit.

dixit is an independent verbal expression, and facturum fuisse is the compound form of the future infinitive. The verbal unit wil be anchored to the token facturum as indirect statement. In the token graph, esse will be related to it as auxiliary.

circumstantial participle

Participles in a circumstantial sense rather than purely attributive sense.

Example: Anco regnante Lucumo, vir inpiger ac divitiis potens, Romam commigravit.

commigravit is an independent verb. The participle regnante (agreeing with Anco) is circumsstantial (“while Ancus was reigning…”)

Counter example: gloria est consentiens laus bonorum. The verb est is independent with subject gloria and predicate laus. Here, the participle consentiens has an attributive sense with laus, “universal praise”. This does not constitute a verbal expression. (In the syntax graph, this will be treated like any other attributive or adjectival expression, and will be related to laus as adjectival.)

When it is unclear whether to take the relation of a participle as attributive or circumstantial, prefer the treatment as circumstantial participle.

Example ille moriens, cum sciret sagittas hydrae Lernaeae felle tinctas quantam uim haberent ueneni, sanguinem suum exceptum Deianirae dedit.

The participle moriens agrees with ille, and the participle tinctas agrees with sagittas. Treat both as circumstantial participles definiing a verbal expression.

Semantic type

arsgrammatica recognizes four semantic types for verbal expressions.

linking verb applies to verbs joining a subject and predicate nominative expression (a substantive or adjectival expression). intransitive is for verbs that cannot take a direct object. transitive active and transitive passive apply to verbs that, in the active voice, are capable of construction with a direct object.

Note that in Latin, transitive active expressions can be converted to equivalent transitive passive expressions under the A-A-T model (and vice versa) by changing the voice of the verb, and the construction of any explicit agent and target as follows:

Semantic type Agent Action Target
transitive active subject active voice direct object
transitive passive ablative of agent passive voice subject

linking verb

The most common verb linking a nominative subject with a nominative predicate is sum.

Example: Etruria erat vicina.

The verb erat is a linking verb with subject Etruria and predicate vicina.

intransitive

Active- or passive-voice verbal expressions that are intransitive in sense (i.e., that cannot take a direct object).

Example: principes Albanorum in patres, ut ea quoque pars rei publicae cresceret, legit.

The verb cresceret is an active-voice form that cannot take a direct object. It is intransitive.

transitive active

Any active-voice verbal expression with a transitive sense, so is capable of taking a direct object.

Example: principes Albanorum in patres, ut ea quoque pars rei publicae cresceret, legit.

The verb legit is transitive active with an explicit direct object principes.

transitive passive

Any passive-voice verbal expression with a transitive sense, so is capable of taking a construction with the ablative of agent.

urbs a Romulo condita est

The compound verb form condita est is transitive passive with grammatical subject urbs and agent expressed by the prepositional phrase a Romulo.

Handling implied verbal expressions

The discussion of tokenization introduced the idea of implied verbal expressions. There are two circumstances where we need to create an entry in the list of verbal expressions for an implied verbal expression.

Omitted forms of sum

Example: omnia praeclara rara.

This is a complete sentence, with a subject phrase omnia praeclara and a predicate rara. We record the implied form of “to be” with a new token entry, and enter its ID into the list of verbal expressions.

and its ID will be entered in the list of verbal expressions.

This predicate construction is a main sentence, so its syntactic type will be independent clause and the semantic type will be linking verb. This elision can also occur with multiword compounds such as the perfect passive system, but here the treatment differs from the bare-predicate case above: because the participle itself is a real, already-present token, it – not a newly created token – anchors the verbal expression, per the multi-word compound verb form rule above. Example: the sentence inde P. Valerius iterum T. Lucretius consules facti. has a single verbal expression with a perfect passive verb, facti with sunt omitted. facti itself is entered in the list of verbal expressions (syntactic type independent clause, semantic type transitive passive). We still add a new token with unique ID and None for its text value to record the elided sunt, but this new token is not itself a verbal expression: it relates to facti as its auxiliary, exactly as a written-out sunt would. One important category of elided forms of sum is the participle: since there is no present participle of sum, its presence is always implied only. Example: in the sentence P. Valerius anno post Agrippa Menenio P. Postumio consulibus moritur, the phrase Agrippa Menenio P. Postumio consulibus is a predicate construction implying a circumstantial participle of “to be”. Here there is no already-present participle to anchor the verbal expression (unlike facti above), so we must add a new token with unique ID to the token table and use its ID for a new entry in the list of verbal expressions. The syntactic type will be circumstantial participle and the semantic type will be linking verb since this is a predicate construction (“when Agrippa Menenius and Publius Postumius were consuls…”)

Understood repeated verb

Especially common in indirect discourse. 2. continuation of indirect discourse: in long sections of indirect speech, entire passages may appear without repeating any governing verb of speaking or thinking. In this situation, we must add new token with unique ID to the token table, with None for its text value, and use its ID for new entry in the list of verbal expressions. For example, these three verbal expressions are each constructed in indirect statement: nimium Tarquinios regno adsuesse; initium a Prisco factum; regnasse dein Ser. Tullium. They imply a verb of speaking governing all three verbal expressions, so we add a new token with unique ID to the list of tokens, and add a verbal expression with its ID to the list of verbal expressions. Here, we assume that the understood verbal expression is of syntatic type independent clause; its semantic type is ‘transitive active’.
